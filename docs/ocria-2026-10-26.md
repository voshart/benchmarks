# OCRia and structured-document evidence update — 2026-10-26

## H100 is the comparison unit

OCRia-1's main comparison speed is an explicitly assumed **0.3–6 pages/second on one
H100 80GB**. Its original B200 measurements remain source observations in Details,
not the main speed cell. The reference has `status: estimated`, `evidence: assumed`,
`confidence: low` and `hypothetical: true`. It does not establish downloadable weights,
a successful H100 port, a measured H100 runtime or hosted API throughput.

The starting observation is **8.67 pages/second at 16 concurrent requests** in the
technical report's Table 20. This matches the reference profile's concurrency ceiling;
we do not substitute the 10.21 pages/second result at 64 requests. All six original
concurrency observations remain intact, including the distinct 7.42 result at eight
requests in Table 20 rather than 7.83 from the separate Table 21 experiment.

NVIDIA's specification sheets give 3.35 TB/s for the H100 SXM and 64 TB/s over eight
B200 GPUs, or 8 TB/s per GPU. A bandwidth-only sensitivity anchor is therefore:

```
8.67 × (3.35 / 8) = 3.6306 pages/second
```

That is **not** a measured conversion or a universal throughput ratio. The source
uses a custom mixed NVFP4/FP8 target, BF16 vision encoder, speculative draft and custom
kernels. An H100 cannot be assumed to execute the native NVFP4 path unchanged. The
source pages also use a different image budget from the common reference workload.

We apply deliberately broad, judgmental runtime/precision/workload factors of
0.08–1.65 to that anchor and round the resulting 0.2904–5.9904 range to **0.3–6**.
The low end allows substantial loss of source-stack optimizations; the upper end
requires a useful compatible speculative implementation and a favourable reference
workload. BF16/FP16 fallback is assumed rather than silently promising FP4. The source's
321–333 end-to-end tokens/second stock-BF16 baseline illustrates why runtime uncertainty
is material. These factors are editorial scenarios, not empirically fitted bounds,
confidence intervals or guarantees. Performance may fall outside them.

The geometric midpoint remains a sorting aid, not an independently established best
measurement. Changing this assumed range cannot change quality rankings. Hypothetical
local-compute cost remains in Details and cannot replace a hosted service price.

## Separate measurements and configurations

The OCRia report checkpoint and OpenDocRouter recipe are separate model configurations,
sharing a ranking identity rather than inheriting each other's results. The latter's
84.67 is a five-category mean, while the report's ParseBench panel excludes grounding.
OpenDocRouter belongs to LlamaIndex and is not independent corroboration.

The report's four-category ParseBench, its 1,288px olmOCR runs, its published-baseline
olmOCR comparison, French PDF evaluation and cropped-table PulseBench are separate
protocols. All comparison rows are retained. The published-baseline panel is diagnostic;
reused Mistral and olmOCR-2 rows inside the 1,288px panel are context-only. The PDF's
LightOnOCR-3 value of 82.1 is retained with the announcement's conflicting 81.2 noted.

Higher-precision announcement ParseBench observations remain separate from the rounded
PDF values. For otherwise tied evidence from this paired publication, the announcement
observations precede the rounded table, consistently across models and irrespective of
whether their scores increase or decrease. Missing metrics are never borrowed. PDF
source-native ratio values are converted exactly to the displayed percentage scale.

PulseBench's mean excludes unparseable predictions. Coverage, median and perfect-table
counts are retained. Its cropped-table components are diagnostics, not new votes in the
end-to-end table rating. Charts and semantic formatting have their own diagnostic view,
without a combined rating or a claim to schema-level key-information extraction.

The complete October 23 OmniParse open-model cohort is included with its stated
pipelines and deviations. Its **table-cell family** is an eligible table component;
its mixed **table-tag composite** remains excluded. Missing layout and headline values
for models without boxes stay absent. The Hunyuan serving section identifies version
1.5 despite the shortened headline label. The ambiguous Indra version is retained as a
separate context-only source-run identity, not silently mapped to a canonical checkpoint.

OpenDocRouter's $13.04 per 1,000 pages remains a workload estimate, its token tariffs
remain tariffs, and its 2.2-second median latency remains latency. None is treated as
an H100 measurement or inverted into aggregate throughput.

## Evidence and validation

The source ledger is `data/ocr/research-ocria-20261026.json`. It records every imported
numeric table, model mapping, the original speed ladder and the H100 assumptions.
Regression tests check completeness, protocol isolation, missing components, source
qualifications, estimate/observation separation and speed-independent quality ranks.
The existing full build and overall/component ranking audits remain the release gate.
